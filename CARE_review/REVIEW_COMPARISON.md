# 1. Bottom line

The two reviews were produced by genuinely different methods — a 648-record automated pipeline over PubMed and OpenAlex, and a narrow hand-verified review of ~30 primary sources plus guidelines, cost studies and burden datasets. They **agree on more than they disagree on**, which is worth something: convergence between methods that share no retrieval path is a stronger signal than either alone.

Where they differ, the pattern is consistent and explainable:

- **The deep-dive is better on implementation texture.** It has country platform detail, procurement failure points, incentive distortions, case-finding-versus-conversion, and a combination analysis that no hand-built review could reproduce. This is the material CARE actually needs to design something.
- **The independent review is better on evidence quality control, normative status, cost and targeting.** It corrects several effect-size framings, adds the WHO 2023 wasting guideline and the Cochrane MMS review, supplies cost-per-DALY, and grounds geography in burden data rather than research volume.

**Neither is the right document to send CARE on its own.** The combined summary should take the deep-dive's implementation spine and the independent review's numbers, costs and targeting.

# 4. What each has that the other lacks

## 4.1 Only in the deep-dive — and worth carrying forward

This is the material that makes a co-design document useful rather than merely correct.

- **Case-finding versus conversion to treatment, decomposed.** Burundi: clinic-based systematic screening enrolled 98% of detected cases against 8% for community mass screening. Mali: screening coverage +40 points while treatment coverage stayed at 7.6%. *This is the single best original framing in either review.*
- **Country platform descriptions with operational specifics** — Ethiopia's ~40,000 salaried Health Extension Workers plus the Women's Development Army; Nepal's 50,000+ Female Community Health Volunteers; Pakistan's Lady Health Workers covering ~60% of the population; India's ASHA/Anganwadi/ANM convergence at Village Health and Nutrition Days.
- **Procurement failure points, named.** Six of eight Indian facilities with insufficient IFA stock; 14 of 24 Nepali facilities out of stock in a nationwide shortage; Pakistan's quota-based rather than demand-based supply to Lady Health Workers.
- **Incentive distortion as a mechanism.** ~Rs.600 per referred institutional delivery skews ASHA effort toward incentivised tasks and away from counselling and home visits; irregular payment damages community trust.
- **Sustainability after donor exit.** Kenya's Baby-Friendly Community Initiative: coverage declined once donor funding ended, with absent county budget lines and unpaid volunteer status as the named threats.
- **The WHO technical correction on MMS safety.** The old "MMS raises neonatal mortality where births are at home" signal was overturned — corrected RR 1.05 (0.85–1.30) against an original 1.22 (0.95–1.57) — and the residual attributed to **iron dose**, producing a concrete design rule: choose an MMS formulation whose iron dose matches current practice. *Neither trivial nor widely known; the independent review missed it.*
- **The combination analysis.** Of 648 on-topic records, 173 discuss combining interventions and **nine actually test one**. A hand-built review cannot produce this number.
- **The Bangladesh adherence drop-off.** The largest coverage loss — 61 percentage points — occurs not at antenatal attendance or IFA receipt but between receiving any IFA and consuming the recommended 180 tablets.
- **Health-system strengthening for early initiation**, RR 2.76 (1.96–3.88) in South Asia, against RR 1.48 for behavioural interventions alone.
- **CHW diagnostic quality** — 75–97% correct MUAC diagnosis with supervision — and integration into iCCM.
- **Twin-publication detection.** The pipeline caught one 42-study evidence base published twice in the CMAM chapter.

**Caveat before reuse:** these claims passed the pipeline's verifier, which checks traceability to a corpus record but **not** whether the population, comparator or certainty survived into prose — the failure mode behind conflicts 1, 6 and 9. Before any of them goes into a CARE-facing document I would spot-verify the load-bearing ones at source. I would prioritise the Burundi 98%/8% figures, the WHO technical correction, the RR 2.76, and the Bangladesh 61-point drop-off.

## 4.2 Only in the independent review

- **Cost throughout** — CMAM $26–42/DALY (Bangladesh, Malawi), Ethiopia community care roughly 2× more cost-effective than inpatient, ComPAS $918 vs $1,041 per child recovered, Pakistan's counter-case where community delivery cost *more* per child recovered; MMS $21–42/DALY with a marginal commodity cost of about half a US cent per tablet.
- **Burden-based targeting.** Low birthweight is genuinely concentrated in South Asia (24.8% vs 13.9%) and holds country by country; wasting does not — the South Asian figure is essentially India (18.7%), while sub-Saharan Africa's 5.9% average conceals Niger, Chad, Burkina Faso and Sudan at roughly double it.
- **WHO 2023 wasting guideline** and its recommendation-by-recommendation strength and certainty.
- **Cochrane reviews the pipeline did not retrieve** — MMS (CD004905) and RUTF (CD009000).
- **The untreated counterfactual** (Olofin, HR 11.63) — the number that carries a finance-ministry conversation.
- **Newest protocol evidence** — OptiMA-Niger 2025, MANGO.
- **KMC replication picture** — iKMC and OMWaNA together.
- **Alive & Thrive** — the strongest at-scale breastfeeding evidence anywhere (+36.2 points EBF in Bangladesh, +27.9 in Viet Nam, through two different delivery channels).
- **MMS targeting evidence** — Smith 2017: the advantage over IFA concentrates in anaemic and underweight women and female infants.
- **The IMPROVING transition study** — IFA→MMS implementation across Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Madagascar and Tanzania.
- **A source register** with per-item verification status.

# 5. Why they diverge

Three structural causes, all diagnosable:

1. **Retrieval boundary.** PubMed and OpenAlex do not reach WHO guidelines, UN burden datasets or organisational reporting — so the deep-dive could not see the 2023 wasting guideline, the JME data, or the current WHA targets. It also missed the Cochrane MMS and RUTF reviews, which it *should* have retrieved; that is a search-strategy gap rather than a boundary one.
2. **Verification checks traceability, not fidelity.** Every conflict in rows 1, 3, 6, 9, 10 and 11 above passed the pipeline's verifier. A number can be traced to a real record and still be attached to the wrong population, the wrong indicator or the wrong claim.
3. **Corpus-bounded claims stated as world facts.** "The only country running CMAM at national scale", "the only high-certainty finding" and "four of five independent syntheses" are all true *of the corpus* and false of the world.

None of this makes the deep-dive unreliable as a body of work. It makes it a document whose implementation content is strong and whose headline effect claims need a verification pass — which is what the independent review supplies.

# 6. Proposed spine for the CARE summary

Roughly 8–10 pages. Draws numbers and framing from the independent review, implementation texture from the deep-dive.

| § | Content | Primary source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What was asked, and how we answered it.** Two independent reviews; note that convergent findings are the confident core and flag where they differed | Both |
| 2 | **The three interventions, stated honestly.** Corrected effect sizes with populations and certainty. KMC as a preterm/LBW intervention with a strong WHO recommendation; CMAM against its real counterfactual; MMS on birth size, not survival | Independent |
| 3 | **What each buys.** Cost per DALY, cost per child, commodity cost of the MMS switch | Independent |
| 4 | **Where.** Burden-based targeting — India and the Sahel, the Horn of Africa and Yemen for CMAM; South Asia for MMS and the newborn package — with the sub-national caveat | Independent |
| 5 | **Why it doesn't scale.** Case-finding vs conversion; supply chain; incentives; workload; sustainability after donor exit; relapse and post-discharge | **Deep-dive**, with independent review's WHO 2023 framing |
| 6 | **Three packaged options.** Corrected: Option A as small-and-sick-newborn care; Option B with the severest-children caveat on simplified protocols; Option C with the targeting rule and iron-dose design rule | Both |
| 7 | **What a ministry will ask.** Normative status — WHO 2023 makes CHW delivery conditional/very-low-certainty; MMS is research-only; simplified protocols run ahead of the guideline | Independent |
| 8 | **What we don't know**, and what would change the answer | Both |

**Three editorial rules for the merge:**

1. **Where they conflict, the independent numbers win** — each was verified at source, and §3 records why.
2. **Every deep-dive implementation claim carried across gets spot-verified first.** The four named in §4.1 are the priority.
3. **Populations travel with effect sizes.** Every risk ratio in the combined document states who it was measured in. That single rule prevents the largest error class found in the deep-dive.

**One open question for you before we draft:** how much of the disagreement to show CARE. My instinct is a short, unfussy note in §1 that two reviews were run and differed on specific points, with the corrections simply applied in the text rather than litigated — partners want the answer, not the audit trail. But if this is also feeding the methods paper, the audit trail is the interesting part and belongs in an annex.
