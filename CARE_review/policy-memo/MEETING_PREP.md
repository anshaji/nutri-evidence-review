# Open with this

> The evidence is not the constraint. Adherence and coverage are. What separates these three
> interventions is not how well they work but **how they behave in a budget** — and that is what
> determines whether a ministry can act on them.

Then the three shapes:

- **Wasting treatment** — same budget, **13% more children recovered**. An efficiency gain.
- **MMS** — a **higher** unit cost buying a better birth. Not more-for-the-same.
- **Kangaroo care** — no commodity at all. A protocol change, not a budget line.

If you say nothing else, say that. It reframes "which is best" into "which fits your situation."

# The six traps

Where the natural answer is wrong. These are the ones that cost credibility.

| If asked | Do **not** say | Say |
|---|---|---|
| Does MMS save lives? | "It cuts mortality 12%" | **No.** It cuts low birthweight 12%. Mortality shows **no effect at high certainty** |
| Who does kangaroo care help? | "Newborns" | **Preterm and low-birthweight infants** — about 1 birth in 7 |
| How much do simplified protocols save? | "50% of cost" | 50% of the **food**; food is ~43% of cost. Measured saving is **12%** |
| Does cash save money? | "It pays for itself" | It roughly **doubles** cost per child and buys much better outcomes |
| Is community delivery cheaper? | "Yes" | Not automatically. In Pakistan it cost **more** per child recovered |
| Does breastfeeding support cut deaths? | "By 25%" | That belongs to **multi-part packages**. Its own share cannot be isolated |

# Questions from policy makers

**"Is this WHO-endorsed?"** — Different answer for each, and this matters.

- **Kangaroo care: yes.** Strong recommendation, high-certainty evidence, 2022. The only
  unambiguous one.
- **Simplified protocols: no.** WHO's 2023 guideline retains weight-indexed dosing (150–185
  kcal/kg/day) and dual discharge criteria. Adopting a simplified protocol means operating ahead
  of guidance. Defensible on the trial evidence, but raise it up front rather than have it
  discovered.
- **MMS: no.** WHO recommends it "in the context of rigorous research." **Daily iron-folic acid
  remains the recommended standard of care.** UNICEF is nonetheless accelerating introduction in
  seven African countries, which gives cover — but do not say WHO recommends it.
- **CHW delivery of wasting treatment: yes, but weakly.** Conditional recommendation on **very
  low certainty** evidence.

**"What does it cost?"** — Ask which denominator they mean; they are not interchangeable.

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| Per child **treated** (wasting) | $135–290 |
| Per child **recovered** (wasting) | $145–380; ComPAS trial $918–1,041 in its settings |
| Per DALY averted, wasting | $26–42 |
| Per DALY averted, MMS | $21–42 |
| MMS commodity gap | **$1.42 per pregnancy** ($2.00 → $3.42) |
| Kangaroo care | No commodity. Ward readiness **$31,000–96,000 per hospital** |

*Cost per DALY is for comparing across interventions. Cost per child is for building a budget. A
finance ministry will want the second.*

**"Why should we move money from what we're already doing?"**

- **Wasting:** you are not moving money. Same budget, ~13% more children recovered — measured in
  a randomised trial, not modelled.
- **MMS:** not a reallocation but a **substitution** in a channel that already exists. No new
  service, visit or cadre.
- **Kangaroo care:** a protocol change. The cost is ward reconfiguration, not recurrent spend.

**"What's the evidence quality?"** — Name the certainty; it varies enormously and saying so
builds trust.

- Kangaroo care, mortality: **high**
- MMS, low birthweight: **high**. Small-for-gestational-age: **moderate**
- Breastfeeding support: **moderate**
- CHW delivery of wasting treatment: **very low** (WHO's own rating)

**"Isn't UNICEF already doing MMS?"** — In seven priority countries, targeting 9.3 million women.
That is an argument for **alignment**, not duplication: the supply-chain and advocacy groundwork
may already exist where they are working.

**"What if you're wrong?"** — Three named risks, not a shrug:

1. Applying simplified protocols to the most severe children. The standard protocol did better
   for MUAC under 115 mm or oedema.
2. Running decentralised wasting treatment and a breastfeeding contact schedule **on the same
   frontline workforce**. Adding a task to a loaded platform displaces an existing one.
3. Selling MMS on mortality. It will not survive technical review.

# Questions from country directors

**"What does my team do differently on Monday?"**

- **Wasting:** retrain on the simplified protocol for uncomplicated cases; keep the standard
  protocol for severe ones. Extend supply chains past the facility to community health workers.
- **Breastfeeding:** replace general promotion with a **specified contact schedule**. Stop
  debating cadre — across 116 trials there was no differential effect by who delivers, though
  power was limited.
- **MMS:** this is advocacy and supply chain, not service delivery. Get it onto the national
  essential medicines list.

**"We already do breastfeeding programming. What changes?"** — The message becomes a schedule.
Cochrane finds contact intensity is what predicts success, with four to eight visits the
tentative optimum. Alive & Thrive raised exclusive breastfeeding 36 points in Bangladesh and 28
in Viet Nam — through an NGO network in one and government facilities in the other, which is the
best evidence that the channel is substitutable.

**"We have limited MMS capacity."** *(Mercy Corps flagged this.)* — It needs the least technical
capacity of the three. The commodity substitutes into existing antenatal care; the work is
policy, procurement and adherence, not clinical.

**"How long until we see something?"** — MMS and kangaroo care can show process change within a
budget cycle (commodity switched, protocol rewritten). Coverage and outcome change takes longer.
Do not promise mortality change on any timeline; two of the three do not demonstrably produce it.

**"Which country?"** — Wasting concentrates in **India** (18.7%, stable system, integration at
scale) and in **the Sahel, the Horn and Yemen** (surge caseloads, where simplified protocols
matter most). **South Asia** is the strongest match for both MMS and the facility newborn
package. But say plainly: the decisive factors are not in any literature — government appetite,
policy windows, fragility, financing. **The evidence says where these have the most to work
with. It cannot say where they can be built.**

# The two hostile questions

**"There's a trial showing kangaroo care doesn't work."** — They mean OMWaNA (Uganda, 2,221
neonates, 2024). Correct answer:

> OMWaNA tested starting **before stabilisation** and found no reduction in early neonatal
> mortality. The 32% figure is kangaroo care against conventional care — 11 trials, 10,505
> infants, high certainty — and that is not in doubt. What OMWaNA questions is whether immediate
> initiation adds further benefit in that setting. WHO's recommendation stands.

**"Your numbers have changed since the last version."** — Answer it head-on:

> Yes. We ran an independent fact-check against primary sources and corrected 17 items, including
> one of our own estimates that turned out to be roughly double the trial-measured figure. Every
> number in front of you now traces to a named source. That is why this version is the one to use.

That converts the weakness into the strongest credibility signal you have. Do not be defensive
about it.

# Three questions to put back to the room

1. **Which of the three shapes matches your funding position** — flat budget, marginal increase,
   or protocol change only?
2. **Do you have a policy window in the next twelve months?** MMS needs an essential-medicines-list
   decision; simplified protocols need a protocol revision.
3. **Is your frontline workforce already loaded?** If so, options 1 and 4 compete, and that
   changes the sequencing.

---

# If you don't know

Say so, and say what would settle it. Three data layers would sharpen any country decision:
**sub-national wasting prevalence**, **maternal anaemia**, and **facility delivery coverage**.
None is in the review; all are obtainable.
