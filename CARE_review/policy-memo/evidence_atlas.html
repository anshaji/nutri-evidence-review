Nutrition evidence review · CEGA

Twenty-three interventions

We retrieved and extracted 1,000 studies on nutrition for children under five and women of reproductive age in low- and middle-income countries, kept the 496 that were on topic, and synthesised them into 23 intervention appraisals. This is what that evidence base contains — and where it came from.

The marked rows are the only ones independently verified. That verification found 33 corrections across two rounds — wrong populations, GRADE ratings asserted where the source assigned none, effect sizes attached to the wrong outcome, superseded survey data. Every correction in the first round had passed the automated verifier, which checks that numbers trace to a corpus record but not that they carry the right population, comparator or vintage. The twenty unmarked rows have not had that treatment.

What this can and cannot tell you

  • It describes a corpus, not a field. Retrieval targeted intervention evidence across twelve domains and was capped at 1,000 records. Country counts here say how often a country appears in the studies we synthesised — not how much nutrition research a country hosts, and not whether research is allocated well.
  • A country mention is not a study conducted there. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are 232 of the 496 on-topic records. A review naming twelve countries counts in all twelve.
  • Study counts are unweighted. A cluster-randomised trial and a cross-sectional survey count alike.
  • Country tags are model-extracted at 74% coverage, and the missing quarter is unlikely to be random — older and non-open-access records are likelier to lack retrievable full text.
  • Burden is point prevalence, not annual caseload. Wasting is acute and recurrent; the number of children affected across a year is materially higher than the snapshot shown.
  • Survey years differ by country and some predate major shocks. Each country's panel states its own year.
