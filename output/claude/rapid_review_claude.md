## 2. Tier 1 Interventions

*Strong evidence (A) + proven scalability + high/very high cost-effectiveness*

### 2.1 Vitamin A Supplementation (Children 6–59 Months)

**Evidence Rating: A**

The most extensively studied child survival intervention in the database, supported by multiple Cochrane reviews and large meta-analyses spanning over 1.2 million children.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| All-cause mortality (fixed-effect) | RR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93); 19 trials, n=1,202,382 | PMID 35294044 (Cochrane 2022), high-certainty |
| All-cause mortality (random-effects) | RR 0.76 (95% CI: 0.66–0.88) | PMID 28282701 (Cochrane 2017) |
| All-cause mortality (6–59 mo) | RR 0.75 (95% CI: 0.64–0.88); 9 studies | PMID 21501438 (2011), fulltext |
| Diarrhoea-specific mortality | RR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.79–0.98); 9 trials, high-certainty | PMID 35294044 |
| Diarrhoea incidence | RR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.82–0.87); 15 trials | PMID 35294044 |
| Measles incidence | RR 0.50 (95% CI: 0.37–0.67); 6 studies | PMID 28282701 |
| Night blindness | RR 0.32 (95% CI: 0.21–0.50) | PMID 35294044 |
| Xerophthalmia | RR 0.31 (95% CI: 0.22–0.45) | PMID 21868478, fulltext |
| Vitamin A deficiency | RR 0.71 (95% CI: 0.65–0.78); high-certainty | PMID 35294044 |

**Subgroup Findings (from full-text papers):**
- Asia subgroup shows larger mortality reduction: RR 0.69 (95% CI: 0.61–0.79) vs. Africa RR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.73–0.98) [PMID 21868478]
- Effect attenuated in recent large trials: well-concealed trials only RR 0.91 (95% CI: 0.85–0.97); trials since 2000 RR 0.96 (95% CI: 0.89–1.03) [PMID 35584136, fulltext]
- Nepal-specific modelling: only 3 fewer deaths per 1,000 children supplemented in current context [PMID 35584136]

**Important Caveats:**
- Neonatal VAS shows **no overall benefit**: RR 0.97 (95% CI: 0.89–1.06) at 6 months across 11 trials, n=163,567 [PMID 30425075, fulltext IPD]. Benefit confined to Asia (RR 0.87, 95% CI: 0.77–0.98) and populations with high maternal VAD prevalence (RR 0.87, 95% CI: 0.80–0.94). Possible harm in Africa (RR 1.06, 95% CI: 0.98–1.15).
- VAS in infants <6 months: no benefit (RR 1.05, 95% CI: 0.88–1.26) and increased bulging fontanelle risk (RR 1.55, 95% CI: 1.05–2.28) [PMID 21975770]
- Adverse effect: vomiting within 48 hours (RR 1.97, 95% CI: 1.44–2.69) [PMID 35294044]

**Mechanism:** Vitamin A maintains epithelial barrier integrity, supports immune cell differentiation, and enhances mucosal defence against enteric and respiratory pathogens.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. One of the most cost-effective child survival interventions globally at ~$1–3 per child per year for biannual supplementation. Scaling to near-universal coverage could prevent an estimated 823,000 annual under-5 deaths [PMID 26869575, abstract-only].

**Scalability:** Proven national. Delivered through national immunisation days, child health weeks, and routine health contacts. Over 80 countries have national VAS programmes.

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Integrate with existing EPI/immunisation platforms; leverage community health worker networks for biannual delivery. However, as under-5 mortality and VAD prevalence decline, the absolute benefit per child decreases — periodic reassessment of programme necessity is warranted.

### 2.2 Iron-Folic Acid Supplementation in Pregnancy

**Evidence Rating: A**

Supported by >20 meta-analyses across the database, including multiple Cochrane reviews. Universal policy recommendation in virtually all LMICs.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Maternal anaemia (IFA vs FA alone) | RR 0.52 (95% CI: 0.41–0.66); 5 studies, GRADE moderate | PMID 32075071, fulltext |
| Iron supplementation → anaemia (children) | RR 0.55 (95% CI: 0.44–0.70) | PMID 31973225, fulltext |
| LBW (iron vs placebo) | RR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.78–0.99); GRADE high | PMID 32075071, fulltext |
| Haemoglobin increase (iron in pregnancy) | MD +7.80 g/L (95% CI: 4.08–11.52) | PMID 32075071 |
| Folic acid → mean birthweight | MD +0.37 kg (95% CI: 0.18–0.43); 9 studies | PMID 31680411, fulltext |
| Folic acid → LBW | OR 0.59 (95% CI: 0.47–0.74) | PMID 31680411 |
| Nutrition education → IFAS compliance | OR 2.80 (95% CI: 2.04–3.83) | PMID 39545365, fulltext |
| Nutrition education → anaemia prevalence | RR 0.66 (95% CI: 0.54–0.80) | PMID 39545365 |

**Intermittent vs. Daily Supplementation (from Cochrane reviews):**
- Similar birth outcomes: LBW RR 0.82 (95% CI: 0.55–1.22); birthweight MD +5.13 g (NS) [PMID 26482110]
- Fewer side effects with intermittent: RR 0.56 (95% CI: 0.37–0.84) [PMID 26482110]
- Better adherence with intermittent: RR 1.60 (95% CI: 1.34–1.91) [PMID 39780191, fulltext]
- Daily produces marginally higher Hb: MD +0.24 g/dL (95% CI: 0.12–0.35) [PMID 39780191]

**Compliance Challenge:** Pooled IFAS compliance in Sub-Saharan Africa is only 39.2% (95% CI: 30.8–48.3%) [PMID 33852614, fulltext]. Key determinants: counselling on IFAS (OR 1.96), knowledge about anaemia (OR 5.42), and ANC visit frequency.

**Mechanism:** Iron corrects iron deficiency anaemia, supporting oxygen transport and fetal growth. Folic acid prevents neural tube defects and supports DNA synthesis critical for rapid placental and fetal cell division.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. IFA tablets cost ~$0.50–2.00 per pregnancy. Among the lowest cost-per-DALY-averted interventions.

**Scalability:** Proven national. Part of standard ANC packages in nearly all LMICs. Main bottleneck is compliance/adherence rather than supply.

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Strengthen ANC platforms with nutrition counselling (which triples compliance); consider intermittent regimens where side effects limit adherence; integrate with mHealth reminders [PMID 30609297, abstract-only — limited evidence but promising].

### 2.3 Multiple Micronutrient Supplementation in Pregnancy (MMS vs. IFA)

**Evidence Rating: A**

Three Cochrane reviews (2017, 2019) and multiple IPD meta-analyses consistently show MMS is superior to IFA for birth outcomes.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Low birthweight | RR 0.88 (95% CI: 0.85–0.91); 18 trials, n=68,801, high-certainty | PMID 30873598 (Cochrane 2019) |
| Small-for-gestational-age | RR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.88–0.97); 17 trials, moderate-certainty | PMID 30873598 |
| Very preterm birth | RR 0.81 (95% CI: 0.71–0.93); 4 trials | PMID 30873598 |
| LBW (all studies) | RR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.77–0.93); 28 studies, GRADE high | PMID 32075071, fulltext |
| Stillbirths (MMS vs IFA) | RR 0.91 (95% CI: 0.86–0.98); 22 studies | PMID 32075071 |
| LBW in anaemic women (Hb <110) | RR 0.81 (95% CI: 0.74–0.89); p-interaction 0.049 | PMID 29025632, abstract-only |
| 6-month infant mortality in anaemic women | RR 0.71 (95% CI: 0.60–0.86) | PMID 29025632 |
| Neonatal mortality — female neonates | RR 0.85 (95% CI: 0.75–0.96) | PMID 29025632 |
| UNIMMAP formulation → LBW | RR 0.74 (95% CI: 0.61–0.90); 11 studies | PMID 32075071 |

**No significant effect on:**
- Perinatal mortality: RR 1.00 (95% CI: 0.90–1.11); high-certainty [PMID 30873598]
- Neonatal mortality: RR 1.00 (95% CI: 0.89–1.12); high-certainty [PMID 30873598]
- Maternal anaemia vs. IFA: RR 1.04 (95% CI: 0.94–1.15) — MMS does not outperform IFA for anaemia [PMID 30873598]

**Important Safety Signal:** Sensitivity analysis excluding one Indonesian trial showed significantly increased early neonatal mortality: OR 1.40 (95% CI: 1.08–1.82) [PMID 20120796, fulltext]. This finding has not been replicated in subsequent larger analyses but warrants monitoring.

**Effect Modification:**
- Birthweight gain concentrated in mothers with BMI ≥20: +39 g; nullified in BMI <20: −6 g (interaction p<0.001) [PMID 20120795, fulltext]
- Greater benefit with early initiation (<20 weeks) and high adherence (≥95%) [PMID 29025632]
- Gestational age assessment method does not materially alter findings [PMID 37002655, fulltext]

**Mechanism:** Addresses multiple concurrent micronutrient deficiencies (iron, folic acid, zinc, vitamins A/B/C/D/E) that limit fetal growth through independent and synergistic pathways.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. MMS costs ~$1.50–3.50 per pregnancy — marginally more than IFA but prevents 12% more LBW births.

**Scalability:** Proven national. WHO conditionally recommended transition from IFA to MMS in 2020. Several LMICs (Bangladesh, Tanzania, Burkina Faso) have begun national rollout.

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Replace IFA with MMS in ANC supply chains. Use existing procurement and distribution infrastructure. UNIMMAP formulation standardises composition across manufacturers.

### 2.4 Large-Scale Food Fortification (Iron, Iodine, Folic Acid)

**Evidence Rating: A**

One landmark systematic review (PMID 30997493, fulltext) covering 136 studies across three decades provides comprehensive evidence for the impact of mandatory fortification programmes.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Nutrient/Vehicle | Outcome | Effect | Source |
|-----------------|---------|--------|--------|
| **Iron** (flour, sauce) | Anaemia (all ages) | RR 0.66 (95% CI: 0.59–0.74) | PMID 30997493, fulltext |
| Iron | Anaemia (WRA) | RR 0.66 (95% CI: 0.58–0.76) | PMID 30997493 |
| Iron | Anaemia (pregnant women) | RR 0.73 (95% CI: 0.64–0.84) | PMID 30997493 |
| Iron | Iron deficiency | RR 0.42 (95% CI: 0.32–0.56) | PMID 30997493 |
| **Iodine** (salt) | Iodine deficiency (school-age) | RR 0.25 (95% CI: 0.21–0.29) | PMID 30997493 |
| Iodine | Goitre prevalence | OR 0.26 (95% CI: 0.16–0.43) | PMID 30997493 |
| **Folic acid** (flour) | Neural tube defects | OR 0.59 (95% CI: 0.49–0.70) | PMID 30997493 |
| Folic acid | Anencephaly | RR 0.49 (95% CI: 0.40–0.60) | PMID 30997493 |
| Folic acid | Spina bifida | OR 0.66 (95% CI: 0.53–0.82) | PMID 30997493 |
| Folic acid | Folate deficiency (WRA) | RR 0.20 (95% CI: 0.15–0.25) | PMID 30997493 |
| **MMN fortification** (various foods) | Anaemia | RR 0.68 (95% CI: 0.56–0.84) | PMID 31849042, abstract-only |
| MMN fortification | Iron deficiency anaemia | RR 0.28 (95% CI: 0.19–0.39) | PMID 31849042 |
| MMN fortification | Vitamin A deficiency | RR 0.42 (95% CI: 0.28–0.62) | PMID 31849042 |
| **Double-fortified salt** (iron+iodine) | Anaemia | RR 0.59 (95% CI: 0.46–0.77) | PMID 29767699, abstract-only |
| DFS | IDA | RR 0.37 (95% CI: 0.25–0.54) | PMID 29767699 |

**Mechanism:** Adding micronutrients to widely consumed staple foods (flour, salt, oil, sugar, condiments) provides population-wide coverage without requiring individual behaviour change.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. Large-scale fortification costs $0.05–0.50 per person per year — among the highest-return public health investments. Copenhagen Consensus ranks fortification as a top-3 development investment.

**Scalability:** Proven national. Salt iodisation covers >120 countries. Flour fortification mandated in >80 countries. Infrastructure leverages existing food-processing industry.

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Establish mandatory fortification standards through food safety regulation; monitor compliance through national quality assurance systems; leverage existing mill/processor infrastructure.

### 2.5 Exclusive Breastfeeding Promotion (0–6 Months)

**Evidence Rating: A**

Supported by the landmark Lancet Breastfeeding Series (PMID 26869575, 7,924 citations) and multiple Cochrane reviews.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Scaling to near-universal: deaths averted | 823,000 under-5 deaths/year preventable | PMID 26869575, abstract-only |
| Under-5 mortality per 1% EBF increase | 3.4 per 1,000 children/year reduction | PMID 33622796, abstract-only |
| 10% EBF improvement → economic savings | ~$1 billion in SSA | PMID 33622796 |
| Undernutrition (breastfeeding vs. not) | OR 0.82 (95% CI: 0.68–0.99) | PMID 35844158, abstract-only |
| Stunting (breastfeeding vs. not) | OR 0.73 (95% CI: 0.55–0.95) | PMID 35844158 |
| GI infection reduction (6 mo EBF vs. 3–4 mo) | Significant reduction (multiple studies) | PMID 22895934 (Cochrane), abstract-only |
| Cryptosporidium protection (breastfeeding) | OR 0.40 (95% CI: 0.13–1.22) — suggestive | PMID 29879110, fulltext |

**EBF Duration:** Cochrane review (PMID 22895934, 1,699 citations) confirms no growth deficits with 6-month vs. 3–4-month EBF, with significant reduction in gastrointestinal infections. Iron supplementation needed in settings with suboptimal newborn iron stores.

**Mechanism:** Breast milk provides complete nutrition, passive immunity (IgA, lactoferrin), and growth factors; EBF reduces pathogen exposure through contaminated food/water.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. Breastfeeding promotion through health systems is extremely cost-effective (cost per DALY averted $1–5 in many settings).

**Scalability:** Proven national. Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative implemented in >150 countries. Requires policy support (maternity leave, marketing restrictions) and health worker training.

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Enforce the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes; expand BFHI; train community health workers in breastfeeding counselling; enact maternity protection legislation.

### 2.6 Complementary Feeding Education and Food Provision

**Evidence Rating: A**

Consistently supported across 4 meta-analyses and 2 Cochrane/systematic reviews in the database (PMID 21501443, 24564534, 28904113, 32710657).

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Weight gain | SMD 0.40 (95% CI: 0.02–0.78) | PMID 24564534 |
| LAZ | SMD 0.11 (95% CI: 0.01–0.22) | PMID 28904113, abstract-only |
| **Education alone — food insecure** | | |
| HAZ | SMD 0.25 (95% CI: 0.09–0.42) | PMID 24564534, fulltext |

| Weight gain (provision) | WMD 0.34 SD (95% CI: 0.11–0.56) | PMID 21501443, fulltext |

**Mechanism:** Education improves feeding practices (meal frequency, dietary diversity, responsive feeding). Food provision directly addresses caloric and micronutrient gaps during the critical 6–24 month window.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Education-only interventions are low-cost; food provision more resource-intensive but justified in food-insecure settings.

**Scalability:** Proven subnational. Delivered through ANC/postnatal contacts, community health workers, and IYCF programmes. National programmes exist in India (ICDS), Bangladesh, and Ethiopia.

### 2.7 Micronutrient Powders (Home Fortification)

**Evidence Rating: A**

Two Cochrane reviews (2011, 2020 update) with 29 RCTs and 33,147 children provide high-certainty evidence.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Anaemia (MNP vs. placebo) | RR 0.82 (95% CI: 0.76–0.90); 16 studies, n=9,927, moderate-certainty | PMID 32107773 (Cochrane 2020), abstract-only |
| Iron deficiency | RR 0.47 (95% CI: 0.39–0.56); 7 studies, high-certainty | PMID 32107773 |
| Haemoglobin | MD +2.74 g/L (95% CI: 1.95–3.53); 20 studies | PMID 32107773 |
| Ferritin | MD +12.93 μg/L (95% CI: 7.41–18.45) | PMID 32107773 |
| IDA (MNP vs. placebo, 2011) | RR 0.49 (95% CI: 0.35–0.67) | PMID 21901727, abstract-only |
| MNP vs. daily iron — diarrhoea | RR 0.52 (95% CI: 0.38–0.72) — less diarrhoea with MNP | PMID 32107773 |
| MNP efficacy — anaemia | RR 0.76 (95% CI: 0.69–0.84) | PMID 31973225, fulltext |
| MNP efficacy — IDA | RR 0.45 (95% CI: 0.34–0.58) | PMID 31973225 |

**Adherence:** Pooled high-adherence rate: 63.3% (95% CI: 51.1–74.6%). Strongest predictor of adherence: absence of side effects (OR 2.77, 95% CI: 2.46–3.11). Knowledge of MNPs and social norms also significant [PMID 34658128, fulltext].

**Mechanism:** Sachets of encapsulated iron + zinc + vitamin A (minimum) sprinkled onto prepared foods at point of use, avoiding palatability issues of liquid iron.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** High. ~$3.60 per child per year for 60 sachets.

**Scalability:** Proven national. National programmes in Bangladesh, Nepal, Kenya, Pakistan. WHO recommends for populations where anaemia prevalence >20%.

### 2.8 Preventive Zinc Supplementation (Children)

**Evidence Rating: A**

Two Cochrane reviews (2014, 2023 update) with 80–96 RCTs and >200,000 children.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Diarrhoea incidence | RR 0.87 (95% CI: 0.85–0.89); 26 studies, moderate-certainty | PMID 24826920 (Cochrane 2014), abstract-only |
| All-cause mortality | RR 0.93 (95% CI: 0.84–1.03); 16 studies, high-certainty | PMID 36994923 (Cochrane 2023), abstract-only |
| LRTI mortality | RR 0.86 (95% CI: 0.64–1.15); moderate-certainty | PMID 36994923 |
| ALRI by specific clinical criteria | IRR 0.65 (95% CI: 0.52–0.82) | PMID 20156999, abstract-only |
| Pneumonia morbidity | RR 0.81 (95% CI: 0.73–0.90); 6 RCTs | PMID 21501441, fulltext |
| Diarrhoea morbidity | RR 0.87 (95% CI: 0.81–0.94); 14 trials | PMID 21501441 |
| Height (growth) | SMD −0.09 (95% CI: −0.13 to −0.06); very small positive effect | PMID 24826920 |

**No significant mortality benefit** demonstrated at high certainty. Benefits primarily in morbidity reduction. Adverse effect: vomiting (RR 1.29, 95% CI: 1.14–1.46).

**Mechanism:** Zinc supports immune function, gut epithelial integrity, and cellular growth. Reduces duration and severity of diarrhoeal and respiratory episodes.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** High. ~$1–2 per child per year.

**Scalability:** Proven subnational. Integrated into child health programmes in multiple countries. WHO recommends in areas with high zinc deficiency prevalence.

### 2.9 Zinc for Diarrhoea Treatment

**Evidence Rating: A**

Cochrane review (PMID 27996088, fulltext) with 33 RCTs and 10,841 children.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Diarrhoea duration (children >6 mo) | MD −11.46 hours (95% CI: −19.72 to −3.19); low certainty | PMID 27996088, fulltext |
| Diarrhoea persisting to day 7 (>6 mo) | RR 0.73 (95% CI: 0.61–0.88); moderate certainty | PMID 27996088 |
| Duration in malnourished children | MD −26.39 hours (95% CI: −36.54 to −16.23); high certainty | PMID 27996088 |
| Persistent diarrhoea duration | MD −15.84 hours (95% CI: −25.43 to −6.24); moderate certainty | PMID 27996088 |

**Critical subgroup finding:** No benefit in children <6 months — duration MD +5.23 hours (95% CI: −4.00 to 14.45) and increased vomiting risk (RR 1.54, 95% CI: 1.05–2.24) [PMID 27996088].

**Mechanism:** Zinc restores intestinal mucosal integrity and enzymatic function disrupted by diarrhoeal illness, accelerating recovery.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. Part of WHO-recommended ORS + zinc treatment protocol. Zinc tablets cost ~$0.50 per treatment course.

**Scalability:** Proven national. Included in WHO/UNICEF guidelines for diarrhoea management. Implemented nationally in >40 countries through IMCI protocols.

### 2.10 Management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (CMAM/RUTF)

**Evidence Rating: A**

Multiple systematic reviews (PMID 24564235, 31906272) covering SAM treatment protocols.

**Key Effect Sizes:**

| Outcome | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| RUTF vs. CSB — SAM recovery | RR 1.51 (95% CI: 1.04–2.20) | PMID 24564235, fulltext |
| WHO protocol vs. standard care — mortality | RR 0.52 (95% CI: 0.43–0.64) — 48% reduction | PMID 24564235 (citing Lancet 2008) |
| RUTF weight gain vs. CSB | MD +1.27 g/kg/day (95% CI: 0.16–2.38) | PMID 24564235 |
| RUTF vs. F100 (community) weight gain | MD +5.5 g/kg/day (95% CI: 2.92–8.08) | PMID 31906272, fulltext |
| Antibiotics + RUTF → mortality | RR 0.74 (95% CI: 0.55–0.98); 3 studies, moderate-certainty | PMID 31906272 |
| Antibiotics + RUTF → recovery | RR 1.06 (95% CI: 1.03–1.08); high-certainty | PMID 31906272 |
| Antibiotics → hospitalisation | RR 0.89 (95% CI: 0.82–0.98); high-certainty | PMID 31906272 |

**Mechanism:** RUTF (peanut-based, energy-dense, micronutrient-fortified paste) provides 500 kcal/92 g with complete micronutrient profile in shelf-stable, ready-to-eat format suitable for home-based care.

**Cost-Effectiveness:** High. CMAM costs ~$200 per child treated, with >75% recovery rates. Cost per DALY averted estimated at $42–53 in high-burden settings.

**Scalability:** Proven subnational. National CMAM programmes in Ethiopia, Niger, Nigeria, Pakistan, and others. Local RUTF production established in several countries and shown equivalent to imported [PMID 24564235].

**Government Scaling Pathway:** Integrate into primary health care; train community health workers for screening (MUAC) and follow-up; establish local RUTF production. Routine antibiotics (amoxicillin) should be included — they reduce SAM mortality by 26%.

## 4. Tier 3 Interventions

*Promising evidence (B or C+) + plausible scaling pathway*

---

### 4.1 Balanced Protein-Energy Supplementation in Pregnancy

**Evidence Rating: B**

Birthweight improvement: SMD 0.20 (95% CI: 0.03–0.38); 7 RCTs, n=2,367 [PMID 25857334, fulltext]. No significant effect on birth length or head circumference. Effect larger when no control supplement given (greater nutritional deprivation). Evidence base small.

---

### 4.2 Calcium Supplementation in Pregnancy

**Evidence Rating: B**

Pre-eclampsia reduction (calcium alone): RR 0.30 (95% CI: 0.17–0.52); 3 studies [PMID 32075071, fulltext]. No effect on LBW (RR 0.99) or preterm birth. WHO recommends in populations with low calcium intake.

---

### 4.3 Vitamin D Supplementation in Pregnancy

**Evidence Rating: C+**

LBW reduction: RR 0.40 (95% CI: 0.23–0.71); 3 trials — but limited to 5 small trials, mostly high-income [PMID 22742603, fulltext]. Maternal serum 25(OH)D dramatically improved: SMD 1.52 (95% CI: 0.98–2.07) [PMID 40752545, fulltext]. Preterm birth (strictly vitamin D): RR 0.33 (95% CI: 0.17–0.62) in 2 studies [PMID 32075071]. Evidence insufficient for LMIC-specific policy.

---

### 4.4 Agricultural Interventions

**Evidence Rating: C**

Improved dietary diversity in 19/23 studies but no significant effect on anthropometric outcomes or haemoglobin [PMID 22251864, fulltext]. Biofortification and home gardens show promise for vitamin A intake but growth evidence weak.

---

### 4.5 Growth Monitoring and Promotion

**Evidence Rating: C**

Very low-certainty evidence of benefit. No clear effect on WAZ or HAZ across 6 studies [PMID 37823471, fulltext]. Home-based GMP may modestly improve WAZ: MD 0.183 (95% CI: 0.037–0.328) [PMID 37823471].

---

### 4.6 Egg-Based Supplementation

**Evidence Rating: C+**

Emerging evidence from sub-Saharan Africa (7 studies, n=3,673):
- WAZ: MD 0.33 (95% CI: 0.11–0.55) [PMID 42137196, fulltext]
- WHZ: MD 0.30 (95% CI: 0.12–0.48) [PMID 42137196]
- HAZ: MD 0.05 (95% CI: −0.05 to 0.14) — no effect on linear growth [PMID 42137196]

**Evidence Rating: B+ (niche population)**

## 5. Cross-Cutting Findings

### Pattern 1: The Anaemia Paradox — Iron Deficiency Explains Only a Fraction

Iron deficiency accounts for only **25% of anaemia in preschool children** and **37% in women of reproductive age** — far below the commonly assumed 50% [PMID 27827838, fulltext]. In high-burden settings (>40% anaemia prevalence, rural), iron deficiency explains even less (14–16%). This means iron-focused strategies alone cannot resolve the anaemia burden; inflammation, malaria, helminth infections, and other micronutrient deficiencies must be addressed concurrently.

### Pattern 2: The Compliance Bottleneck

Across interventions, efficacy consistently outstrips effectiveness due to poor adherence:
- IFAS compliance in SSA: only 39% [PMID 33852614]
- MNP adherence: 63% overall, 44% in non-China LMICs [PMID 34658128]
- Side effects are the strongest barrier to adherence (OR 2.77 for MNP non-compliance with side effects)

**Nutrition education consistently doubles or triples compliance** (OR 2.80 for IFAS, PMID 39545365), making it a force-multiplier for any supplementation programme.

### Pattern 3: Effect Modification by Nutritional Status at Baseline

Multiple interventions show **greater benefit in more deprived populations:**
- MMS: birthweight gain nullified in mothers with BMI <20 but +39 g in BMI ≥20 [PMID 20120795]
- MMS: LBW reduction stronger in anaemic women (RR 0.81 vs. 0.88 overall) [PMID 29025632]
- Zinc for diarrhoea: MD −26 hours in malnourished children vs. −11 hours overall [PMID 27996088]
- VAS: greater mortality benefit in high-VAD populations (RR 0.87 vs. 1.05 in low-VAD) [PMID 30425075]

This argues for **targeted rather than universal** supplementation in settings where deficiency prevalence is declining.

### Pattern 4: Nutrition-Specific vs. Nutrition-Sensitive — Complementary, Not Substitutable

The evidence base shows clear limits to nutrition-specific interventions alone:
- WASH alone has only borderline HAZ effects (SMD 0.08–0.15) [PMID 31272479, 23904195]
- Agricultural interventions improve diet but not anthropometry [PMID 22251864]
- CCTs reduce stunting (HAZ +0.20–0.43) through multiple pathways including food, health services, and behaviour change [PMID 31666032]
- **76% of studies combining multisectoral policies with nutritional supplementation successfully reduced stunting**, vs. variable results for single-sector approaches [PMID 31666032]
- Risk factor analysis confirms the multi-causality: diarrhoea (OR 1.77), low birthweight (OR 2.40), and low maternal education (OR 1.67) are consistent independent predictors of undernutrition [PMID 40886100, fulltext]

### Pattern 5: The First 1,000 Days Window Is Real but Not Exclusive

Most interventions show strongest effects during pregnancy and 6–24 months, but the evidence base identifies important gaps:
- **Adolescent nutrition**: very limited evidence (10 studies, all Asian LMICs, low quality) [PMID 31878019, fulltext]. Iron may improve Hb (MD 0.42 g/dL) but uncertain effects on anaemia or growth.
- **School-age children**: iron supplementation improves cognition (SMD 0.50) and reduces anaemia ~50% [PMID 24130243]
- **Elderly malnutrition**: 22.7% prevalence in Ethiopia; low dietary diversity increases odds 4-fold [PMID 37771505, fulltext]
- **HIV-positive adults**: nutritional interventions significantly improve BMI (SMD 0.80) and CD4 count (SMD 0.99) [PMID 40460180, fulltext]

### Pattern 6: Evidence Quality Varies Dramatically by Intervention

| Intervention | Highest GRADE | Number of Cochrane Reviews | Total RCTs in Evidence Base |
|-------------|--------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| VAS (child 6–59 mo) | High | 3 (2011, 2017, 2022) | 47 trials, >1.2M children |
| MMS in pregnancy | High | 2 (2017, 2019) | 20 trials, 141,849 women |
| MNP (home fortification) | High (iron deficiency) | 2 (2011, 2020) | 29 trials, 33,147 children |
| Zinc (preventive) | High (mortality) | 2 (2014, 2023) | 96 trials, 219,584 children |
| WASH → nutrition | Low | 1 (2013) | 5 cluster-RCTs |
| Adolescent nutrition | Very low | 0 | 10 studies, 10,802 |
| Agricultural interventions | Very low | 0 | 23 studies (mostly non-randomised) |

## 6. Methodology Notes

### Evidence Strength Rating Criteria

- **A (Strong):** ≥2 high-quality meta-analyses or Cochrane reviews with consistent findings; high or moderate GRADE certainty; large combined sample sizes (>10,000)
- **B (Moderate):** 1–2 meta-analyses with moderate-quality evidence; some inconsistency across studies or populations; smaller sample sizes
- **B+ (Moderate-Strong):** Between A and B; strong direction of effect but limited by sample size, study number, or GRADE rating
- **C (Emerging):** Limited meta-analytic evidence; low or very low GRADE certainty; small number of trials; inconsistent findings
- **C+ (Emerging-Promising):** Between B and C; suggestive positive direction but insufficient for confident recommendation

### Cost-Effectiveness Rating

- **Very High:** <$100 per DALY averted or established as best-buy by Copenhagen Consensus/DCP3
- **High:** $100–500 per DALY averted; well-established delivery platforms
- **Moderate:** $500–1,500 per DALY averted; requires dedicated programme infrastructure
- **Low:** >$1,500 per DALY averted or insufficient CEA evidence

### Scalability Rating

- **Proven national:** Implemented at national scale in ≥3 LMICs with documented coverage
- **Proven subnational:** Successful district/state-level implementation with evidence of feasibility
- **Growing:** Pilot programmes with positive results; scaling pathway identified but not yet proven
- **Requires investment:** Theoretical feasibility but no large-scale implementation evidence

### Key Limitations

1. **43 of 100 papers were abstract-only** — effect sizes from these papers could not be independently verified against full-text results sections and may represent selective reporting
2. **Geographic concentration:** Evidence disproportionately from South Asia (India, Bangladesh, Nepal) and East/West Africa. Latin America, Central Asia, and Pacific Island LMICs are underrepresented.
3. **The database contains no dedicated CEA-tier papers** despite the search design including a CEA category — cost-effectiveness ratings therefore rely on external knowledge and are approximate.
4. **Publication bias** was documented in several meta-analyses (IFAS compliance, folic acid/LBW, anaemia interventions) and may inflate effect estimates.
5. **Heterogeneity** was high (I² >75%) in many pooled analyses, limiting confidence in point estimates.
6. **One paper (PMID 36321557) is a protocol only** — no results were available for extraction.
7. **Paper PMID 34100558** (early postnatal discharge) and **PMID 35864290** (preoperative nutrition in cancer surgery) are tangential to the core LMIC nutrition intervention question but were included due to composite scoring.
